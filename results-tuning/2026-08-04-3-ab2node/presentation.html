RCCL default vs tuned config

A 13-rule tuner config measured against stock RCCL across 18 message sizes. 1 of 13 rules survives. This is a negative result and the negative result is the finding: at 7 of the 18 sizes RCCL’s 2-node default already selects the exact algorithm, protocol and channel count the sweep proposed — so at those sizes there was nothing to take. The one real win, +11.1% at 32M, comes from changing the algorithm, not from raising the channel count.

Why there was nothing to find

At 4K–64K the config asked RCCL for what RCCL was already doing

The sweep’s answer at the five smallest sizes was tree/ll with 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 channels. The 2-node default is tree/ll with 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 channels. Not similar — identical, in all three fields, at all five sizes. Those rows are a null experiment, which is why two of them come back at exactly +0.0%. Two more sizes land the same way higher up: at 16M and 64M the config requested 48 channels and the default was already at 48, so 7 of the 18 sizes are a config that cannot differ from the default. They are the shaded columns in both charts above.

And the deltas there are literally one printed digit. rccl-tests prints busbw to two decimals. At 16K that last digit is worth 1.27% and the measured change is -1.27%; at 32K the digit is 0.66% and the change is -0.66%; at 64K the digit is 0.35% and the change is -0.35%. Three of the five are a single count in the last column the tool can show, and the other two are dead equal. That is the strongest form the statement “no difference” can take here.

Where the default’s channels come from. Above 64K the 2-node default sits at 48 channels for every remaining size, and the INFO log says why: RCCL caps itself there. A tuner rule asking for fewer than 48 is therefore asking for a downgrade, which is exactly the shape of the results — the eight sizes that lost 2.8–3.9% are all sizes where the config requested fewer channels than 48 without changing the algorithm.

Reading of the evidence (a pattern across the 18 verified rows, not a single logged fact): channel count is not the lever at 2 nodes. The two sizes that requested exactly the default’s 48 — 16M and 64M — came back at -0.5% and -0.4%, inside the resolution floor. Of the six rules that kept the default’s algorithm and protocol and only trimmed the channel count, every one lost 2.8–3.9%. And the only clear gain came from switching algorithm (tree→ring at 32M) while asking for fewer channels, 40 against 48. Changing algorithm or protocol is necessary but plainly not sufficient: 128K and 256K were handed the identical rule, tree/ll128 at 16 channels, and came back -2.8% and +3.3%. If there is more bandwidth to find at 2 nodes, this points at the algorithm and protocol boundaries around 16M–32M, not at the channel count.

RCCL’s 2-node defaults are already well tuned at small message sizes. Its 1-node defaults are not. That single sentence accounts for both results. It also accounts for the missing landmine: the 1-node config lost 65.7% above 64M because it collided with WarpSpeed’s channel multiplier, and WarpSpeed never engages at 2 nodes — 0 RING* rows in all 252 data rows of this A/B, and no WarpSpeed line in any of the 224 per-rank INFO logs. The trap that made the 1-node config dangerous simply is not reachable here, which is why this config is merely useless rather than harmful.

Measurement
  • 2 nodes, amd-mi355x-5 and amd-mi355x-7, both on rail L1 leaves; 8 ranks × 1 GPU per node = 16 ranks.
  • RCCL 2.28.3-develop:2e42aa8 (read from the INFO log), rccl-tests 2.17.9-develop:2e42aa8, 20 iters + 5 warmup.
  • 7 repeats per arm, interleaved default/config so drift cannot favour either arm; 0 hangs in either arm.
  • In-place busbw, median of 7. 0 incorrect elements across all 252 data rows.
Verification
  • Deployed through the RCCL v4 tuner plugin. Firing checked per run: TUNER/Plugin: Using DN-TUNER (v4) in 112/112 config per-rank logs, and no DN-TUNER line in any of the 112 default logs.
  • Selection verified per size from the run’s own algo/proto/channel columns; identical across all 7 repeats at every size in both arms.
  • Keep threshold: P(sup) ≥ 0.95, best gain > 2.0%, and no size in the range regressing more than 2.0%.
  • Both arms ran under identical NCCL_DEBUG=INFO→file logging, measured elsewhere to be free (mean −0.06%), so the comparison is not biased by it.

Three things this page does not claim. First, these busbw numbers are not comparable to the sweep-measured ones: the sweep reaches the GPUs by mpirun and this A/B by srun, which shifts absolute bandwidth. Every comparison here is between the two arms of this A/B only, which were launched identically — the A/B is self-contained. Second, at the smallest sizes busbw is printed to two decimals, so one least-significant digit is worth 5.0% at 4K, 2.5% at 8K and 1.3% at 16K — no difference smaller than that is even representable there. That is why the ±1.3% floor is drawn on the delta chart and why those sizes are reported as unchanged rather than as small regressions. It cuts the other way too: the 11.1% at 32M is 24.7 GB/s on values printed to 0.01, so it is nowhere near this limit. Third, the “never engages” statement about WarpSpeed is scoped to this A/B: 0 RING* rows in 252 rows and no WarpSpeed line in 224 per-rank INFO logs at 2 nodes. It is not a claim about other node counts or other RCCL builds.
