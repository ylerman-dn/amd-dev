How we choose RCCL configs — four approaches, one winner

The problem: per message size (18 of them), pick algorithm + protocol + channel count. Measuring one candidate takes ~30 seconds of cluster time. Question: how to find the best candidates without measuring everything? All numbers below are from replaying every method on the same 15 measured datasets (5 collectives × 1/2/3 nodes).

THE RULER Full grid — measure everything

The ground truth: every candidate, 3 repeats. 434 measurements. Too expensive for routine use, irreplaceable as the judge: every other method below is scored by "did it find what the grid found?"

Kept as the rare "calibration" run: new hardware or new RCCL version → run one grid, verify the cheap method still finds the same winners.

THE WINNER Adaptive (racing) search

Sketch every combo cheaply at 4 anchor points, kill combos that lose everywhere, refine the survivors per size. Found the grid's exact winner at 235 of 236 sizes (worst miss 1.45%) for ~20% fewer measurements — and it stops by itself when the race is decided.

This is what the tool runs today. Its picks are then verified live against RCCL's default through the deployment path before anything ships.

REJECTED Optuna — Bayesian "smart guessing"

Built for huge spaces (thousands of knob combinations, hours per trial) where any improvement within a fixed budget is a win. Our space is tiny (20-54 candidates) and cheap — so guessing has nothing to add: Optuna needed a median ~20.5 of 27 candidates to find all winners; pure random needed ~23.5. And in 2-4 tries out of 10 it never found them at all.

Its fatal flaw for us: it cannot know when to stop. We only knew it had "succeeded after 20" by peeking at the grid answer — which real tuning does not have. It also re-proposed already-tried candidates 788 times out of 810 asks (a known weakness on small discrete spaces).

Fair note: under a relaxed "within 5% is fine" bar it converges in nearly all tries at about half the cost — still without a stopping rule. If our search space ever grows 100×, reconsider it.

THE CONTROL Random order

Not a method — a measuring stick. Any "smart" search must beat a dumb shuffle. Here the shuffle nearly matched Optuna, proving that intelligent ordering is worthless on this problem; the racing search wins by cheap partial information instead (one measurement covers all 18 sizes at once).

DEMOTED Dip triage — "search only where it hurts"

Physics says throughput must rise with message size. A dip in the default's curve is proof of a mistuned size — found for the price of a single measurement. Searching only dip-flagged sizes cost ~90% less…

…but a default can be uniformly slow with no dip: at some sizes the curve looked healthy while a config 59% faster existed. Dips prove guilt; a smooth curve proves nothing.

Its real home: cheap regression monitoring after tuning — one measurement now and then; a new dip = an alarm that something changed.

The one lesson above all

Cheap search is safe only because a live A/B gate stands behind it: whatever any search picks must beat RCCL's default through the real deployment path, reproducibly (P(sup) ≥ 0.95 over 9 interleaved pairs), before it ships. The gate caught rules that measured +small in the search and ran -65% in deployment. No search method replaces it.
