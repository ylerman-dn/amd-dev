How we choose RCCL configs: four approaches, one winner

The problem: per message size (18 of them), pick algorithm + protocol + channel count. One measurement costs ~30s of cluster time and reports all 18 sizes at once. How to find the best candidates without measuring everything? All numbers: replays of every method on the same 15 measured datasets (5 collectives x 1/2/3 nodes).

Where each approach lives in the flow

Optuna and random appear nowhere in this flow: both were evaluated for the SEARCH box and rejected. Triage was evaluated for a "pre-filter" role (shrink the search) and demoted to post-deployment monitoring.

THE WINNER Adaptive (racing) search

Sketch every combo at 4 anchor channel counts, kill combos that lose everywhere by 15%+, hill-climb the survivors per size. Exact grid winner at 235 of 236 sizes (worst miss 1.45%), ~20% fewer measurements, and it stops by itself when the race is decided.

Why it beats guessing: one measurement reports ALL 18 sizes, so a cheap anchor sketch carries huge information. Elimination exploits that; samplers cannot.

REJECTED Optuna, and why it does not fit here

Optuna's TPE shines when the space is huge (thousands+ of combinations), each trial is expensive, and "best found within a fixed budget" is success. Our problem is the opposite on every axis:

Optuna assumesour reality
huge space, cannot enumerate20-54 candidates - CAN enumerate
one objective to maximize18 independent per-size winners; averaging them blurs exactly what we tune
budget = the stopping rulewe need THE winner; it has no signal for "you have found them all"
fresh candidate each trialreal trace: 810 proposals, 788 were repeats of already-tried configs

Measured consequence: median ~20 of 27 candidates to find all winners when it succeeds - and in 2 to 7 tries out of 10 it never finds them (seed 1 never proposed the 4K winner at all, at any budget).

DEMOTED Dip triage - three possible roles, one survives

Physics: throughput must rise with message size. A DIP in the default's curve is proof of a mistuned size, found for one measurement:

roleideaverdict
1. Filter (before search)search only dip-flagged sizes, ~90% cheaper rejected as default: smooth curves hid up to 59% headroom (a default can be uniformly slow with NO dip)
2. Amplifier (after search - the original design)dig deeper where results still look bad redundant: the adaptive search already refines per size
3. Monitor (after shipping)re-measure the default curve occasionally; a NEW dip = something changed (RCCL upgrade, fabric) kept - its best role. One measurement per checkup, zero false alarms by construction
THE RULER Full grid

Measure everything, 3 repeats: 434 measurements across the 15 datasets. Too expensive routinely; irreplaceable as the judge - every claim above is scored against it. Kept as the rare calibration run: new hardware or RCCL version, run one grid, verify adaptive still finds the same winners.

The one lesson above all

Cheap search is safe only because a live A/B gate stands behind it: whatever the search picks must beat RCCL's default through the real deployment path, reproducibly (P(sup) ≥ 0.95, 9 interleaved pairs per arm), before it ships. The gate caught rules that measured "+small" in the search and ran -65% in deployment (WarpSpeed suppression). No search method replaces it.
